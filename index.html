One on one quizzes are great, but running the same quiz across your entire friend group adds a whole new dimension. Suddenly you can see patterns: who knows everyone best, which friendships run deepest, and where the surprising gaps are.

Why Group Scoring Works

When everyone answers the same set of questions about each person, the results become a map of your social circle. You might discover that two friends who rarely talk actually know each other better than expected, or that the group organizer everyone relies on is quietly the least known. Research on friendship shows these bonds thrive on mutual knowledge, and a group quiz makes that knowledge visible.

How to Run a Group Round

  • Everyone writes five questions about themselves with clear answers
  • Collect the questions into one shared quiz
  • Each person answers every other person's questions
  • Score per pair, then build a simple leaderboard

Reading the Results Together

Go through the answers as a group rather than just posting scores. The leaderboard is fun, but the commentary is the point. When someone scores low on your questions, ask what surprised them. You will often find the gap came from never having had that particular conversation, which is an easy fix.

Turn Gaps Into Plans

Use the results as a to do list for your friendship. If nobody knew your favorite hiking trail, plan a group hike. When you compare friendship scores across the whole crew, low scores stop feeling like failures and start looking like invitations. Every blank answer is just an adventure you have not shared yet.

Keep the Tradition Going

Run the group quiz twice a year and watch the leaderboard shift. New friendships will climb fast as people learn each other, and old ones will stay strong at the top. The changing scores become a living history of your circle, written one question at a time.
